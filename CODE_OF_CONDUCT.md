# Code of Conduct

SafeLink is built for people in some of the worst moments of their lives, and it is built in the open by people who are giving their time. We want working on it to be respectful, useful and calm.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good faith. Disagree about the work, not the person.
- Give feedback you would be willing to receive: specific, actionable, and about the change.
- Welcome newcomers and people who write or read English as a second language. Say what you mean plainly.
- Respect other people's time: keep issues and pull requests focused, and say so when you can no longer work on something you claimed.
- Be honest about what works and what does not. Saying "I did not test this on a real phone" is a contribution, not an admission of failure.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or demeaning comments, including about someone's identity, background, language or experience level.
- Publishing someone's private information, or sharing real emergency data from the app or logs that identifies a person.
- Spam, advertising, or trolling in issues, pull requests or discussions.
- Anything else that would be unwelcome in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in this repository (issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, commits) and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a conduct concern privately through the [Security tab](https://github.com/gasthecreator/safelink-sentinel/security/advisories/new) (start the title with "Conduct:"), so it is not discussed in public. The maintainer will read it, may ask you for more detail, and will keep reporters' identities private.

Depending on what happened, the response can be a private word, a request to edit or retract a comment, locking or removing content, a temporary block, or a permanent ban. The maintainer's decision is final, and the maintainer is not exempt: if the concern is about the maintainer, say so in the report and GitHub support can be contacted.

This policy is written for this project and was influenced by the common open-source community codes, notably the Contributor Covenant.
